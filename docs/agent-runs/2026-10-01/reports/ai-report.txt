
################ AI driver brain

== summary
I've built the computer drivers as a new, self-contained module (`js/ai.js`, `F1.createAIDriver` and helpers under `F1.AI`). There are 16 unit tests in `test/ai.test.js`, all passing, and race simulations in `devtests/ai-test/`. No existing file was edited, and nothing was committed, pushed or packaged.

**What it does:**
- **Steering and speed.** Each AI car follows the racing line with analog steering, throttle and brake. One racing line serves every bot; each bot builds its own speed profile from the line's shape and its own car's limits.
- **Skill levels.** There are four levels (新手 / 業餘 / 職業 / 傳奇) plus a mixed field. Alone on track, the median lap over 8 circuits is +7.7 % / +4.8 % / +2.5 % / +1.0 % slower than the reference. The legend ranges from 0.72 to 1.17 %. Lap-to-lap spread is 0.03–0.4 s, and a faster team's car still shows on top of skill.
- **The reference is not the old keyboard calibration driver.** That driver is too crude: it is 3 % slower than the racing line's own prediction at Monaco. The reference is the line's own margins driven perfectly, battery included.
- **Racecraft and recovery.** Bots follow at a safe gap, pass on the side with room, give room alongside, defend mildly, obey blue flags and ignore ghosts. They react at lights out, recover from spins, grass and walls, and ask for the player's R when stuck. Lower skills make occasional seeded mistakes.
- **Pit stops.** Each bot plans stops by weighing tyre wear against the pit-lane time loss. It picks the softest compound that lasts, drives the lane at the limit with the limiter, stops in its own box and exits through the taper.
- **Names.** A lineup helper names bots after the season's real drivers. If a human takes a team's car, the teammate drives the other seat. All 180 cars from 2010 to 2026 have two drivers in the generated data.

**Results:**
- **Big matrix:** 42 races on 14 circuits with 4, 8 and 15 cars, 1246 race laps. Every car finished, with 0 resets, 0 stuck cars and 0 pit-lane speeding.
  - Contacts: 3.5 per 100 car-laps, mostly mild; worst severity 0.32.
  - Finishing order matches skill exactly from a qualifying grid. From reversed or random grids the correlation is only about 0.5.
- **All 40 circuits:** laps alone at both ends of the skill range, plus a pit stop on each — 80 of 80 clean, no offs, no hits.
- **Cost:** `think()` takes about 1.3–2.5 µs per call. Call it every physics step (120 Hz), right before `car.update`; 15 bots cost well under 1 % of a core. Once V8 has optimised it, it allocates about 0.3 bytes per call.

**Limitations:**
- **Passing between similar cars is hard,** because the car physics has no slipstream. To let much faster cars through, a slower bot under pressure from a quicker one moves aside on a straight after a while. Rookies do this after about 3 s and legends never. This is a game device, not real physics.
- **Allocation during the first minutes.** For the first minutes of a race, while V8 is still optimising, `think()` allocates a few hundred bytes per call.
- **Bots don't shield themselves from humans.** A bot never starts contact, but a human turning in on it can still hit it.

**Integration** (rooms, server, session, UI, main loop) is designed in detail below, not built yet.

== openIssues
 - Passing between cars of similar pace is rare because js/car.js has no slipstream and equal cars have equal top speed. A slower driver under sustained pressure from a car of better known pace gives it room on a straight (rookie after 3 s up to pro 14 s; legends never) — a gameplay stand-in for slipstream/DRS that the user may want to know about. From reversed or random grids the finishing order only partly sorts by skill (Spearman ~0.5); from a qualifying grid it is exact.
 - Contacts: ~3.5 per 100 car-laps, mostly mild (closing speed < 6 m/s), concentrated in lap 1 and slow corners. The AI never starts contact, but it cannot protect itself from a human turning in on it. Not tuned against a human driver.
 - Allocation: think() is allocation-free only after V8 has optimised it. In the first minutes of a race (new drivers, rarely taken branches) it allocates hundreds of bytes per call; harmless young-generation GC, but not literally zero from the first step. Views and contexts must come from F1.AI.createView / createContext, or the property loads go megamorphic and box numbers.
 - Qualifying: timing starts at the first crossing from the grid slot, so bots push from their slot (no slow out-lap, which would waste a counted lap). After their laps they park in their box only if the session lasts long enough for them to reach the pit entry; otherwise the grid placement takes over.
 - Data to ship: the driver lists are in devtests/ai-test/drivers.json. The integration must generate js/drivers-data.js (build-drivers.mjs --js) or add drivers to tools/build-cars.mjs / js/seasons-data.js (F1.cars.drivers). Without it, lineup falls back to invented names.
 - Integration not done (rooms, server, session, gp.js offline bots, UI, main loop): the design is in apiNotes. The host-leave policy (bots removed / DNF on a dedicated server) and the server's human-priority rule (a joining human replaces the newest bot in free practice) are proposals.
 - Recalibrate PACE (devtests/ai-test/calibrate.js) after the v6.2 steering-law / banking / track-data changes and after any js/car.js or js/raceline.js change. The AI picks up car.perf.steerLockAt automatically, but its lap gaps per level will shift.
 - Not verified in Electron or with real players: everything was measured in node with the real modules. devtests/ai-test/out/matrix.json is generated output (git-ignore it if wanted).
 - test/server.test.js flaked once in this session (a 'js/net.js against a hostile server' assertion) and passed on two reruns. It is unrelated to this work, but another workflow is editing net/* and js/seasons-data.js concurrently.

################ AI critic

== summary
I reviewed the computer-driver brain (js/ai.js) independently, ran stress races on circuits the author didn't focus on, and fixed the real problems I found. All the author's checks still pass and my own stress suites all pass too, except the wrong-way-car runs, which are reported, not passed (see below). Nothing was committed, pushed or packaged, and I only touched js/ai.js, test/ai.test.js and devtests/ai-test/.

**What I found and fixed:**
1. **Rear-ending at speed.** A car only counted as "in my path" by where it was at that moment. So the AI missed a car the racing line would swing onto, and its braking was too weak when the gap was closing fast. That gave rear-ends at up to 77 km/h of closing speed (Zandvoort, Imola, COTA). Now it checks where the two cars will meet, where both are actually heading, and brakes harder when closing.
2. **Hitting a stopped car hidden behind another car.** Only the nearest car ahead was braked for. A cleanly overtaking car could then run into a parked car at full speed (Red Bull Ring, Spa). Every car on the path now counts.
3. **Whole fields stuck behind a parked car.** Going round a stopped car aimed at the wrong place whenever the racing line swung across the road. Ten cars sat still for minutes, and pressing R put them back behind the wreck, so they looped: 1080 R in 41 runs. Fixed with a proper swerve round the stopped car, single-file queuing under a yellow flag, creeping past once clear, backing off when nose-to-tail, and an R that places the car past the wreck.
4. **The slower car swerving in front of a car it was letting by** (letting a faster car past, and blue flags). It re-picked its side every step. It now picks a side once and holds the edge.
5. **Pit lane.** Two cars stopping on the same lap ignored each other before reaching the pit lane and rubbed side by side for seconds (about 30 contact events at Monza). Fixed.
6. **Tyre strategy, two bugs.**
   - Once a tyre was nearly worn out, the planner gave up and never stopped. At the game's maximum wear (×5) that meant punctures.
   - It also projected wear one lap too pessimistically. At Jeddah the three legends who qualified 1-2-3 made two needless stops each and finished 13th–15th. After the fix they finish 1-2-3.
7. **Memory use (garbage collection).** The other workflow's new steering law made the AI allocate about 47 bytes on every call. It's back under 1 byte per call once warmed up.
8. **Car coming the wrong way, and finished cars.** A car driving the wrong way was only noticed 160 m ahead; now 300 m, and the AI moves aside. Cars that had finished and were heading to their pit box couldn't pass a stopped car; now they can.
9. **Pace levels recalibrated.** The other workflow changed the steering and track data during my session, so I recalibrated the strength levels as the author's procedure requires. The four levels sit at +8 / +5 / +2.5 / +1 % again.

== apiNotes
 - The author's integration design is still valid (script order after js/pit.js; driver names from build-drivers.mjs or folded into build-cars; createAIDriver / think / reset / onPit / startCompound / planStop / pace; one factory-made view per car refreshed in place every step, one context per bot; the per-step loop as in devtests/ai-test/sim.js; createContacts; the online protocol of host-owned bots with messages bots / bs / bgl and hit.from / bot; the room UI controls with labels from F1.AI.LEVELS plus 混合). Only the points below changed or were added.
 - R for a bot: when think() returns input.reset, call `var idx = F1.AI.resetCar(bot.car, track, bot.lap.jumping ? bot.lap.prevIdx : st.sampleIndex, others, bot.id); bot.lap.sync(idx);`. `others` is the same views array passed to think() (views need x, z, speed, sampleIndex, ghost, id) and bot.id skips the bot's own view. The reset point then moves up to ~80 m along the track to the first spot with no solid car within 7 m and no car standing in the 30 m ahead. Without `others` it behaves exactly like main.js's R (keep that one for the player). sim.js and critic-sim.js call it this way.
 - Views: `speed` must be the signed scalar speed (car.state.speed; reversing < 0) and `heading` the car's heading. The AI now uses them to recognise standing cars (|speed| < 3 m/s for > 1.5 s = an obstacle that triggers yellow-flag behaviour) and cars coming the wrong way (negative speed along the track). For remote humans the interpolated snapshot speed is enough. Keep `ghost` true for pit-asphalt / quali / pre-lights cars as before: a ghost never counts as standing.
 - New debug fields on ai.state: tgt = id of the car being passed or driven round (-1), capBy = id of the car whose follow cap binds (-1), obs = sample of the stopped car being swerved round (-1). Optional, e.g. for an F3-style overlay. ai.log notes 'back off', 'R: blocked by a stopped car' and 'stop: worn ...'.
 - Behaviour the game will show (worth a line in the help text):
- A car standing on track (a crashed human included) puts the bots behind it under a yellow flag: single file, no racing past moving cars, no defending.
- They swerve round the stopped car; one that is blocked across a narrow street asks for R after 8 s, and the R above puts it down past the obstacle.
- A car coming the wrong way is avoided from up to 300 m.
- Lapped bots move to one edge (side chosen once) and lift; slower bots let a faster car by after ~2 s (rookie) to ~8 s (pro) of pressure, and legends never do.
 - Steering: when js/car.js has perf.steerLockAt (v6.2, present now), think() reads it from a table built lazily per bank bin (0.01 rad) and turn side, in 0.25 m/s steps, so it never calls the law per step (calling it boxed doubles: 47 B per think). A new perf object (car change) rebuilds the table; nothing for the integrator to do.
 - Recalibration: PACE in js/ai.js is now [[0,0.84],[0.35,0.89],[0.7,0.941],[1,0.977]] (v6.2 steering law + track data as of 18:35). Re-run `node devtests/ai-test/calibrate.js` after the v6.2 integration settles and paste the printed table. Then run `node test/ai.test.js`, `node devtests/ai-test/matrix.js`, `node devtests/ai-test/alltracks.js` and the three critic scripts (critic-start / critic-obstacle / critic-mixed, ~2.5 min together).
 - Tyre strategy is now more conservative: stints planned to end below 88 % wear, a soft counted at 2.4x in planning, in at the latest when the next chance would see > 93 % or the set is past 85 %. At wear x5 (WEAR_MAX) expect 2..4 stops in 10 laps and no punctures; at wear 1..3 short races usually need none.
 - Cost: think() 1.5..3 us per call; < 1 B per call once V8 has optimised it (test/ai.test.js after a 6-min warm-up). In the first 3..6 minutes of a race V8 is still optimising and think() allocates a few hundred B per call, same as the author's version (young-generation GC handles it). devtests/ai-test/alloc.js only warms up for 1 minute, so its 'with the other cars' figure reflects that phase.

== openIssues
 - On-track passing between computer cars is about 25-30 % lower than the author's version (138..150 vs 197 passes in the matrix). The safety rules (every car on the path is braked for, the predicted meeting point is checked) cost some aggressive moves; js/car.js has no slipstream. Finishing order vs skill is back to or above the author's level (median 0.74; reversed grid 0.55, random grid 0.61), but the 8-race reversed-grid sample is noisy (0.1..0.5 between equivalent versions).
 - A car driving the wrong way: bots now see it from 300 m and swerve round the meeting point, but the test's kinematic ghost never yields and is not pushed, so head-on hits remain (about half as many as before). A human driving the wrong way in the game is pushed by contacts and is less extreme. Not judged pass/fail.
 - Throttle/brake chatter while following in traffic: 90..170 transitions per minute vs ~25 alone. It comes from the follow caps and the in-path predictions switching. A pedal band (PEDAL_BAND 1.5 m/s^2) and a 0.3 s in-path hold were added, but most of it remains. It isn't visible in the game (no brake lights; other cars' engine sound guesses throttle from speed), but a physical-looking pedal / brake-light display of bots would show it.
 - The other workflow (v6.2) changed js/car.js (steerLockAt), js/raceline.js, js/track.js and tracks-data.js during this session (latest 18:35). PACE was recalibrated against the state at that time. Re-run devtests/ai-test/calibrate.js once v6.2 is final, and devtests/seasons-calib/calibrate.mjs (~22 min) for test/cars.test.js, which now fails on seasons calibration drift.
 - Car data observation (not AI): in the current seasons data a 2010 HRT laps within ~1 % of a 2026 Mercedes at Monaco, and a 2026 pro in a 2026 car was slower than a 2010 HRT rookie at Mexico. The 2026 cars may be rated too slow.
 - A car stopped on track triggers a yellow flag. Bots queue in single file and pass it one at a time, so with 10 bots the last can wait 10..30 s (counted as 'stuck' by my metric, by design). A car blocked by a wreck that leaves no room asks for R after 8 s, and the R with others puts it past the wreck: a mild gameplay cheat, only after waiting.
 - Erratic human drivers: bots never ram them (4 of 36 contacts, max 0.24), but they cannot avoid being hit from behind or alongside by a blind or brake-testing human (30 of 36 contacts were the human's).
 - Integration (rooms, server, session, UI, main loop) is not built; see the integration notes for the exact steps. Online bots are host-simulated; ownership transfer on host migration is still to do.
