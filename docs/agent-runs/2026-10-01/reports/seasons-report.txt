
################ seasons builder + calibration

== apiNotes
 - window.F1_SEASONS = [{ year, label, engine, paceIndex, era, cars }] for 2010..2026 in ascending order.
 - Season fields: label = year + ' · ' + the eras.json era description (e.g. '2014 · V6 渦輪混合動力第一代：8 速、ERS'). engine = e.g. '1.6 L V6 渦輪混合動力' or '2.4 L V8 + KERS'. paceIndex is additive (the real pole-time index, 2025 = 1).
 - era holds the absolute CarSpec physics, drivetrain, ers and cockpit fields of the season's standard car.
 - cars[] entries: { id, lineage, team, teamZh, car, engine, cylinders, aspiration, colour, colour2, ratings, perf: {power, drag, downforce, grip, brake, traction, ersPower, ersHarvest}, note, est: {lapPct, topKmh} }. The first entry is '<year>-standard' (all multipliers 1, ratings 50, note ''). lineage and est are additive.
 - window.F1_SEASONS_INFO = { generatedBy, f1db: {name, url, release, licence, licenceUrl}, attribution, attributionShort, sources2026, disclaimer }. In node, require('js/seasons-data.js') returns the array.
 - js/cars.js must load after js/seasons-data.js; it does not need js/car.js (index.html order is fine). It also sets module.exports = F1.cars.
 - F1.cars.seasons: frozen, ascending [{year, label, engine, count}]; count includes the standard car.
 - F1.cars.list(year): a new array of CarSpec, standard car first. year may be a number or a 4-digit string; a year that is not a season returns [].
 - F1.cars.get(id): CarSpec or null. Only strings matching /^[a-z0-9-]{1,40}$/ are looked up, and only through a Map.
 - F1.cars.resolve(id, year?): never null. (1) Year missing or not a season -> the id's own year if the id is known, else DEFAULT_YEAR. (2) The id is that year's car -> it. (3) Else the same lineage's car in that year. (4) Else that year's standard car.
 - resolve lineage lookup: a known id uses its lineage. An unknown '<yyyy>-<x>' or bare '<x>' treats x as a constructor id or lineage key; 'standard' means the standard car. So js/cars-data.js ids map to 2026: resolve('mercedes') -> '2026-mercedes'; resolve('racing-bulls', 2012) -> '2012-toro-rosso'; resolve('audi', 2010) -> '2010-sauber'; resolve('2013-caterham', 2016) -> '2016-standard'; resolve('constructor') -> '2026-standard'.
 - F1.cars.DEFAULT_YEAR = 2026. F1.cars.attribution = frozen {f1db (full F1DB CC BY 4.0 text incl. licence URL and 'corrected for F1Drive'), f1dbShort ('F1 data © F1DB (CC BY 4.0), modified'), sources2026, disclaimer (Chinese: estimates, not official)}. The menu should show f1dbShort next to OpenStreetMap, plus the disclaimer.
 - CarSpecs have exactly the contract fields: id, year, team, teamZh, car, engine, colour, colour2, ratings, note, the 7 physics fields, gearKmh, topKmh, rpmIdle, rpmShift, rpmMax, shiftTime, cylinders, aspiration, ers, cockpit.
 - CarSpec values = era x multiplier: power x power, dragK x drag, downforce x downforce, latBase and latMax x grip, brakeBase x brake, traction x traction, ers.power x ersPower, ers.harvest x ersHarvest. Multipliers are clamped to 0.95..1.05; broken numbers fall back to the v5 reference values, so there is never NaN.
 - CarSpecs are built once and deep-frozen (the same object on every call): copy before changing one. F1.sanitizeSpec(spec) deep-equals spec for every car (tested). get('2025-standard') deep-equals F1.REF_SPEC.
 - F1.buildRaceLine(track, perf?): perf = car.perf (F1.carPerf(spec)); omitted = F1.CAR_PERF, and the output is then bit-identical to before. Each line captures its car's constants, so building another car's line does not change an existing line's update().
 - What main.js must do (stage G): (1) Room/GP year y: ui.setCars({year: y, cars: F1.cars.list(y), seasons: F1.cars.seasons, selected}). (2) Own car = F1.cars.resolve(ui.getCar(), y); a car of another year switches with resolve(id, y). (3) On a car change: car.setSpec(spec), line = F1.buildRaceLine(track, car.perf), F1.audio.setEngine(spec), cockpit.setCar(spec, colour). (4) Remote cars: F1.cars.resolve(p.car, net.year) for livery and engine sound. (5) Show F1.cars.attribution.f1dbShort in the menu, the full f1db string on the help page, and the disclaimer.
 - ratings.ers is 50 for cars without a battery (2010, ers null); ui.js already hides that bar when !spec.ers.
 - tools/build-cars.mjs flags: --check (exit 1 when js/seasons-data.js or entries.json differ), --dry, --quiet; about 13 s. It refuses a stale calibration.json (fingerprint of config, REF_SPEC, uncalibrated era physics, index, reference line laps). After any change to car.js, raceline.js, the tracks or eras.json: node devtests/seasons-calib/calibrate.mjs (about 2.5 min), then node tools/build-cars.mjs.

################ seasons data check

== summary
I checked the seasons data and physics independently and fixed four defects. All tests pass. Nothing was committed and `npm run dist` was not run.

1. **Era calibration (the main defect).** I wrote my own driver (`devtests/seasons-calib/check-drive.js`) that drives the real car and racing-line code with analog pedals and steering. On four circuits outside the builder's 12 (Sepang, COTA, Jeddah, Zandvoort), 2014 came out 1.08 % slower than the pace index, 2015 0.96 % and 2026 0.74 %. Over all 40 circuits of the game, 2014 and 2015 were still 0.72 % and 0.66 % off. The cause is the builder's hand-picked 12 circuits: five of them (Monaco, Monza, Singapore, Red Bull Ring, Bahrain) are where the low-downforce seasons look fastest, so the median was biased. The generator now calibrates on every circuit in `tracks-data.js` (the room can race any of them). I re-ran the 40-circuit driven calibration (11 min, 0 off-track runs, all seasons within 0.03 % of the index) and regenerated. Result: within 0.48 % on my four circuits and within 0.16 % over all 40.
2. **2026 Racing Bulls Chinese name.** It showed 小紅牛 (from `js/cars-data.js`), while the fact-checked livery files and every other year of that team use 紅牛二隊. The 2026 Chinese team name now comes from the livery files; the multipliers, notes and colours from `js/cars-data.js` still take precedence.
3. **2026 Mercedes chassis name.** It showed 'W17'. By the same documented rule as 2014-2025 it is now 'F1 W17 E Performance' (Wikipedia: "Mercedes-AMG F1 W17 E Performance").
4. **Corrupted data in `js/cars.js`.** It accepted any positive number from the data file, while `js/car.js` only accepts ¼..4× the reference (plus gear, rpm and battery ranges). A corrupted value would reach the HUD and sound while the physics silently used the reference car. `js/cars.js` now applies `js/car.js`'s exact ranges to the era value and again to each era × multiplier product. A new fuzz test (300 corrupted files, over 3000 cars) fails with the old file and passes now.

Checked and found fine:
- `resolve()` follows its rules on every case I tried, including hostile ids, string or fractional years and all lineage chains. Cars are built once, frozen and shared; `list()` returns a copy. The attribution strings are correct.
- Every documented raw-data correction is applied.
- Every season's champion is the fastest or second-fastest car (second: 2012 Red Bull behind McLaren, 2022 Red Bull behind Ferrari, 2024 McLaren behind Red Bull, all matching real qualifying pace). The backmarkers (HRT, Virgin, Marussia, Caterham and later ones) are last. With my own driver the order matches real qualifying with rank correlation 0.988-1.000, spread 1.20-1.40 % (2026: 1.57 %), no car off the road.
- The eras are plausible: 2014 is slower in corners (184 vs 193 km/h at a 100 m radius) and faster on straights (336 vs 318 km/h) than 2013; 2017 gains 12.7 % in corner speed; 2020 is the fastest year; 2022 is slower than 2021; V8 cars have 7 gears and rev to 18 000; V6 cars have 8 gears and shift at 11 800 (2026: 11 500); 2010 has no battery; KERS 2011-2013 is small (91 W/kg, 6.4 s).

== openIssues
 - Circuit-to-circuit spread is a limit of the car model. Within a season, individual circuits differ from the index by up to about 2.6 % (rms 1.2 % in 2014, 1.34 % in 2026). Real pole times scatter by a similar amount, but the game's pattern matches reality only weakly (correlation 0.21 across 202 real circuit pairs). So a different four circuits can still land about 0.5 % off. On the builder's old 12 circuits, 2026 is now −0.62 % and 2014 −0.49 % (the mirror image of the old bias).
 - 2026 on its real circuits: on the 14 circuits where the real 2025→2026 ratio was measured, the game gives 1.0258 against the real 1.0322. The game's 2026 car is too quick at Monza, Red Bull Ring and Monaco and too slow at Hungaroring, Zandvoort and Barcelona. The car model has one traction cap: 2026's ×1.115 (peak power) also raises tyre grip out of slow corners, although the 2026 tyres are narrower. It also has no 'battery runs out near the end of a long straight'. Fixing this needs a js/car.js model change, which is not my file.
 - 2026 battery taper: the rules cut ERS power from 290 km/h to zero at 345 km/h; js/car.js has no taper, so holding the battery gives a theoretical 380.7 km/h. In practice the 11 s store limits it to about +1 km/h at the end of Monza and +13 km/h at the Hungaroring. A fix needs a speed-taper field in the contract and js/car.js (owned by stage E).
 - 2011-2013 cars reach top speed on their traction limit, not their power (traction 11.11 m/s², power / speed 11.5 m/s² at 318 km/h). Team power multipliers therefore change nothing in those seasons; only traction and drag matter. Lap-time targets and order are still met because the solve uses the real physics. A related note from the plan: a traction multiplier below about 0.965 lowers top speed.
 - Uncertainty of the index itself: its three constructions (chained year to year, direct comparison, overall fit) differ by 0.5-1 % in some years (2018: 0.993 / 1.004 / 0.999; 2026: 1.032 vs fit 1.027). The game follows the chained index.
 - 2026 per-lap harvest is capped at 1.90 stores (rules: 2.125) by js/car.js's 10× ceiling on ers.harvest, keeping 5 % headroom for team multipliers. Already documented.
 - Wording to fix in js/README-interfaces.md (not my file): it says js/seasons-data.js is generated by tools/build-seasons.mjs; it is generated by tools/build-cars.mjs from tools/seasons-raw.json. Also, 'no year -> DEFAULT_YEAR's standard car' is literal only for unknown ids: a known id with no year returns that car itself (tested, intended).
 - Engine display names follow F1DB, for example 2017 Toro Rosso shows as 'Toro Rosso (Renault)' and 2024 Haas as 'Ferrari 066/10'. I checked the Haas one against Wikipedia; the other pre-2024 engine names were not checked one by one.
 - The web-search budget was used up, so the only web check was on Wikipedia pages (Mercedes W17, Haas VF-24). The historical per-circuit pole-time comparisons use the per-circuit data in tools/seasons-raw.json.
