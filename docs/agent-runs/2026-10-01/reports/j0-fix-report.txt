
################ fix: car.js

== fixed
 - car-car-impacts-invisible-to-tyres: new car.bump(strength) in js/car.js, root cause fixed. It keeps the strongest bump since the last update, ignores anything that is not a number > 0 and caps it at 1. At the start of the next update with dt > 0 it sets state.hit = bumpHit, which walls can still raise, and clears it. Every tyres.update sub-step then sees it through load.hit = state.hit, exactly like a wall hit, and the tyres' HIT_NEW / hitPeak logic counts it once. A zero dt keeps a bump waiting. car.reset() drops a bump that has not been fed yet, so a fresh set fitted after a session change cannot be damaged by an old contact. Nothing touches x, z, speed or heading. Proof: the verifier's probe reproduced 0 flat spots and 0 punctures out of 200. The new devtests/car-v6/car-bump.js, which calls car.bump(F1.resolveCarCollisions(...)), gives 200 flat spots and 164 punctures, against 200 and 150 for the wall reference, and 0 values differ in the path without tyres over 240 steps. Regression test 'car.bump: ...' in test/car.test.js checks: the strongest bump reaches every sub-step of the next update only; state.hit; clamping and ignoring bad values; dt 0 keeps it; reset drops it; real tyres with random 0 get a puncture and flat spot from bump(1) and nothing from bump(0.15); 1200 steps with bumps every 7 steps leave every state key except hit bit-identical.
 - sanitize-ers-zero: sanitizeSpec's rule is now as follows. ers === undefined gives the reference battery. An object gives a battery only when at least one of store / power / harvest is a non-NaN number and neither store nor power is 0 (-0 counts as 0); its out-of-range fields then take the reference's values as before. Everything else gives null: null, false, 0, '', NaN, true, 'x', 5, [], {}, {foo: 1}, {store: NaN}, {store: '4000'}, {store:0, power:0, harvest:0}, and a store or power of 0. Proof: probe-ers.js now prints null, battery 0, topBoost 330.0 for 0, '', NaN, {}, 'x' and the all-zero 2010 object; missing still gives the reference battery at 346.2. New test 'sanitised ERS: ...' covers all of these and checks that createCar(spec) has battery 0 and topSpeedBoost === topSpeed. It also covers well-formed and partial batteries, harvest 0 being out of range, and idempotence. The existing garbage-spec assertion ({store:-1, power:'big', harvest:1e12} -> the reference battery) and the build-cars.mjs check that sanitizeSpec leaves the ERS unchanged both still pass.
 - car-pit-paved-not-in-contract: documentation only, as instructed. I did not change the code and added no inLane() fallback, following the verifier: inLane does not cover the tapers, so a fallback would silently turn them into grass. js/car.js's header now says track.pit must also give paved(index, d). Reproduced with probe-paved.js: a pit with only the contract fields throws 'pit.paved is not a function' with the car in the lane. The contract text is in apiNotes for the orchestrator.

== notFixed
 - traction-cap-limits-top-speed: confirmed, deliberately not changed, as instructed, because the seasons data is calibrated with it. probe-traction-top.js reproduces it. The reference car reaches 330.0 km/h; with traction x0.95, power x1.05 and drag x0.95 it reaches 336.3 against 341.3 without the cap. The cap binds below a traction multiplier of 0.9621. Stub tyres with grip.traction 0.9 give 318.6 km/h. New devtests/car-v6/traction-cap.js shows the real shipped data IS affected: 47 of 197 CarSpecs in js/seasons-data.js have a traction-capped top speed. That is all of 2011-2013 and much of 2010, plus 2017-sauber (-0.46 km/h) and 2018-williams (-0.64). They lose 0.07 to 7.62 km/h; the worst is 2011-virgin at 313.1 against 320.7 without the cap. F1.carPerf(spec).topSpeed includes the cap (topFor), so the racing line, the HUD and the physics agree, and the driven calibration saw the capped physics. The coupling through tyre wear is also still there: car.js multiplies the constant drivetrain cap by grip.traction, so heavily worn rears lower top speed. Changing either would change trajectories and trip the build-cars fingerprint and calibration.

== apiNotes
 - main.js, needed for the car-car-impacts fix: inside the fixed-step loop, write `var ch = collide ? F1.resolveCarCollisions(car.state, others, STEP, contacts) : 0; if (ch > 0) car.bump(ch);`. Use only the local detection return value. Do NOT call car.bump for F1.applyCarImpulse / netHit, the impacts other clients report: a hostile client could otherwise puncture other cars. Keep writing the frame's strongest hit into car.state.hit for the HUD, rumble and sound as now; the next update clears it anyway. The bump shows up again in state.hit one step later. audio's HIT_REARM (0.15 s) treats that as the same contact. The pad may rumble one extra frame, which is harmless.
 - Behaviour to know: car.bump(strength) takes 0..1; values above 1 are capped; anything that is not a number > 0 is ignored; the strongest bump since the last update wins. It is fed on the next car.update with dt > 0: the tyres get a flat spot or puncture chance as for a wall hit of that strength (hit > 0.2 flat spot, > 0.4 puncture chance), and it shows in state.hit. car.reset() drops a bump that has not been fed yet. It never changes the car's path.
 - Contract (README-interfaces.md v6), for the orchestrator: (a) in the 'js/car.js additions' list add `car.bump(strength)`: a car-to-car impact 0..1 (main.js, after F1.resolveCarCollisions, local contacts only), fed to the tyres and state.hit on the next update; no effect on the path. (b) README line 46: state.hit is '0..1 wall or car (car.bump) impact strength this step, else 0'. (c) In the track.pit block add `paved(index, d)  // -> bool: on the pit asphalt (lane, boxes, tapers; never the road itself), from..to`. js/car.js calls it without a guard, and test/car.test.js and devtests/pit-test already rely on it; js/track.js is the only producer and always defines it. (d) CarSpec `ers` line: 'null (no battery) | {store, power, harvest}'. F1.sanitizeSpec: missing = the reference battery. null, false, 0, '', non-objects, objects with no numeric store / power / harvest, and store or power 0 = no battery. Other out-of-range fields take the reference's value.
 - js/cars.js owner (not mine): cleanEra line 75 still uses the old rule, where only `ers === null || ers === false` means no battery, so an era written as ers: 0 / {} / {store:0,...} gets the reference ERS there. js/car.js now treats such values as no battery. It is harmless today: tools/build-cars.mjs writes ers: null for 2010, and cars.js always outputs a clean object that sanitizeSpec keeps (test/cars.test.js 'every CarSpec is exactly what js/car.js drives' passes). Mirror the rule there for consistency.
 - traction-cap-limits-top-speed, for the contract / docs: spec.traction is also a drivetrain cap at every speed. Top speed = min(drag/power balance, sqrt((traction - 0.5) / dragK)), and carPerf.topSpeed already includes it. With the reference power and drag, a traction multiplier below 0.962 lowers the top speed. 47 of 197 shipped season cars (2010-2013 mostly) are capped by 0.07 to 7.6 km/h; `node devtests/car-v6/traction-cap.js` lists them. Worn or dirty rear tyres (grip.traction < ~0.962) lower the top speed the same way. Left unchanged on purpose because the calibration was driven with it. Any tool that rates top speed should use F1.carPerf(spec).topSpeed, not a power/drag-only formula.

################ fix: cockpit + carmodel

== fixed
 - cockpit-delta-never-on-slower-track: Cause: cockpit.js kept its own copy of the best lap time (lapBest), and a new track did not clear it. So on a track with longer laps than the previous track's best, no reference lap was ever recorded. Fix: the reference lap is now recorded when the finished lap equals the counter's own best (lap.last <= lap.best). The private copy is removed. A new track brings a new counter, and lap.reset() clears its best. When the counter is restarted (lap.reset() or arm(), or the timing restarting after a void lap), the partial lap recording starts over, and the old reference is dropped once the counter has no best any more. Verified: probe-cockpit-delta.js now prints 'NOT reproduced: DELTA shown on track B'. New regression tests in api.test.js use the real js/laps.js: 80 s laps, then 100 s laps, then 60 s laps, all showing DELTA from lap 2. A lap.reset() case shows LAP on the first lap, then DELTA. I checked that both tests fail against the old logic in a scratch copy.
 - cockpit-mirrors-static-envmap: The glass is now a plain material showing a 512x96 render target, with each glass mapped to its half and flipped left-right like a real mirror. Each glass has a rear-facing camera (a child of cockpit.group), placed by build() at the glass and looking along the driver's reflected line of sight: straight back, 3° down, about 40° wide with the glass's aspect. The extra render runs in the glass mesh's onBeforeRender, inside the frame main.js already renders, so main.js needs no change. It draws one glass per frame, skips a glass the camera cannot see, draws both on the first frame, and is guarded against running inside itself. It hides the glass, the own car's carbon and dark trim, the scenery trees and anything marked userData.mirror === false (remote-car name tags now are). It restores the previous render target and the renderer's statistics setting afterwards. setMirrors(false) or setMirrors(0) goes back to the old environment-mapped glass and frees the target; info().mirrors reports the state. Verified: api.test.js checks the texture mapping and flip for all three cockpit styles, the camera placement, and the render pass through a stub renderer (which glass, what is hidden and restored, only the visible glass when looking 55° left, no pass inside a pass, setMirrors off and on, pass count). Screenshots I read: cars behind show in the correct mirror, a car directly behind shows in both, the own sidepod shows at the inner edge, head-look left and right shows the live glass, the fallback works, Monaco walls and buildings show. Measured with shots.js perf: +0.12–0.22 ms per frame mean (Monza 0.19–0.22, Monaco 0.12–0.16), budget 1.5 ms; with the trees included it would be 0.17–0.27 ms.
 - cockpit-vs-carmodel-halo-height: cockpit.js now exports its halo line as F1.COCKPIT_HALO; the cockpit geometry is unchanged. carmodel.js builds the same curve and draws it as 4 straight tubes a side, chosen to stay within 4.2 cm of the curve, plus the centre pillar from the apex down to the cowl at z = 0.95. That is 9 tubes of 30 vertices as before, so vertex count, colours and draw calls match v5. carmodel.js keeps its own copy of the numbers for when cockpit.js is not loaded. I also added setHalo(on), which collapses the halo's triangles into the helmet, for cars from before 2018. Verified: the api.test.js v5 comparison is updated. Same vertex count and colours as v5, and only the 270 halo vertices moved. Those vertices sit within 3 cm of the cockpit line and the top is at 1.36 m, against 1.33 m for the cockpit's centre line. Loading carmodel.js alone gives an identical halo. setHalo(false) collapses only the halo and setHalo(true) restores it exactly. The remote-side and remote-front34 screenshots show the same hoop shape as the cockpit's own model.
