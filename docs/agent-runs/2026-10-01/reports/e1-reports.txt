
################ net + session + gp (car, year, wear)

== summary
I built the v6 network / session / Grand Prix controller side per the contract in js/README-interfaces.md ('Network, session, Grand Prix controller'), keeping the v5 hardening: every inbound field is validated, sizes are bounded, nothing throws, broadcasts are coalesced.
- net/session.js: start({q, r, len, year, wear}). year must be a whole 2010..2100, otherwise null; it is never clamped, because a clamped year would be a different season. wear is clamped and rounded to 1..5, default 1. The snapshot carries both. 'again' keeps them; 'free' keeps those of the last session, like q / r.
- net/server.js: every player has a `car` (from hello, any phase; '' = not chosen). The roster rows carry it. `profile` updates it only in phase 'free' (parc fermé); name and colour still change during a session.
- net/server.js, room year: new host-only `year {y}` message, accepted only in 'free' and rate-limited exactly like the track message. The year goes out in `welcome` and in every `players` message. It stays on host migration and in an empty room. A host `gp start` puts the room's own year into the session and ignores any client-supplied year. It also forwards `wear`.
- js/net.js: net.year, net.setYear(y) -> bool, and a 'year' event (also fired once after 'connected', before 'track'). setProfile / getProfile gain car; roster and players[i] gain car; hello sends it; gp('start') forwards wear. cleanSession types year and wear. A car chosen during a session is re-sent automatically once the room is back in free practice.
- js/gp.js: start({q, r, year, wear}, len). Offline both go into the local session; online only wear goes through net.gp. view() gains year and wear. cleanSnapshot sanitises both.
- Tests added: session +2 (28 total), gp +3 (19), server +6 (38): validation, host-only, phase rule, rate limit, host migration, snapshot content, garbage input, the real js/net.js against the real server, and a hostile server sending garbage year / car / wear.

################ pit.js

== summary
I wrote js/pit.js (F1.createPit) and test/pit.test.js (22 tests, all passing), and checked the module against the real track.pit on every track, in node and in Chromium.

- **The module:** pure logic, classic ES5 IIFE. No DOM, no THREE, no timers. It works as a browser script (window.F1.createPit) and in node (module.exports = createPit), and the random generator can be injected.
- **Visits:** entering and leaving the lane comes from track.pit.inLane(sampleIndex, d). Positions are counted along the lane from the entry line in lap order, so a lane across the start/finish line works when the sample index wraps.
- **Speeding:** more than 3 km/h over the lane limit, flagged once per visit. It adds 5 s to a pending hold. The next stop serves the whole pending hold, whether it happens in this visit or a later one.
- **Service:** it starts when the car is at rest (under 0.5 m/s) in its own box, at most once per visit. The tyre change takes a random 2.0 to 4.5 s, mostly 2.2 to 3.2 s.
- **Robustness:** 'exit' only fires once the car is 6 to 8 m past a line on two updates in a row. So a car sitting on a line, reversing a little over it, or a one-step index glitch does not fire events twice. Every 'enter' gets exactly one 'exit'.

The fuzz test found a real flaw in my first design: a car reversing back in after 'exit' could get 'speeding' outside a visit. I fixed it.

What I checked:
1. **Unit tests** against stub lanes: straight across the start line, the same lane not wrapping, and a 160 m-radius curved lane. They cover everything on the task list. 16 deliberately broken copies of pit.js were each caught.
2. **Real tracks:** devtests/pit-logic/real-track.js drives the lane on all 40 tracks, locating the car with track.locate as car.js does. On the 39 tracks that have a real lane, the car stops where pit.js's own boxAhead reaches 0: within 0.3 m of the box point and 5° of its heading. It gets its service and exits, and a run through the lane too fast gives exactly one penalty. The one track without a lane (mc-1929) is inert.
3. **Browser:** an Electron page check loads the module in Chromium with no script errors and passes.

**What the real track.js does with boxes:** box (x, z) is the centre of the painted box, and the yellow stop bar for the nose is 2.85 m ahead of it. With main.js's CAR_NOSE of 2.8 m, the car's origin stops 5 cm from the box point. So pit.js treats the box point as the car's position, which is the default (boxBack = 0).

**Cost:** each update takes at most about 0.3 µs, and the heap did not grow over 2 million updates.

################ tyres.js

== summary
I built the tyre model in js/tyres.js and its test suite in test/tyres.test.js. All 13 tests pass. I also added verification harnesses under devtests/tyres-test/. The model follows the contract (createTyres, fit, setWearRate, update, state) and is pure logic: no DOM or THREE, it works in the browser (window.F1) and in node (module.exports), it is deterministic given random(), and update() allocates nothing (checked with V8's GC trace).

Model. Each tyre's work each step is u^2 * a(v) * v, where u is the share of grip in use and a(v) is the reference car's limit at that speed. Cornering work goes 55 % to the fronts and 72 % of each axle to the outside tyre. Braking goes 64 % to the fronts, traction all to the rears. Sliding adds slip * a_lat(v) * v * 4, mostly on the tyres that slide. Rolling adds 0.25*v per tyre and grass 12*v. The fronts wear 1.55 times faster for the same work because they are narrower.
- **Wear:** each step adds rate * compound wear * 3.9e-6 * work * dt, raised further when the tyre is overheated.
- **Grip vs wear:** no loss up to 20 % wear, 0.8 % at 50 %, 4 % at 75 %, 12 % at 90 %, 31 % at 100 %.
- **Punctures:** a tyre worn past 100 % punctures somewhere between 103 % and 112 %, drawn when the set is fitted. A heavy impact has a chance of 0.8*(hit-0.4)/0.6. One puncture at a time, cured only by fit().
- **Temperature:** hard racing keeps tyres at 90-99 °C, inside every compound's window (S up to 105, M 110, H 115). Sustained sliding overheats them: the grip drops and they cool in 5-10 s at speed. There are no cold tyres.
- **Dirt:** grass dirties the tyres (-12 % grip). They clean in about 150 m of asphalt, 3.3 s at 45 m/s.
- **Flat spots:** heavy sliding over slip 0.4 (mostly lock-ups under braking) or an impact over 0.2 leaves one. It never heals, vibrates more with speed and costs up to 6 % braking grip on that tyre.
- **Car-level multipliers:** lateral grip is weighted towards the loaded outside tyres, braking towards the fronts (62 %), traction on the rears. All are continuous; a new, clean medium set gives exactly 1 / 1 / 1.
- **Compounds:** soft +1.5 % grip and twice the wear, hard -1.5 % and half the wear.

Measured stint lengths:
- **All 40 tracks,** the reference car on the racing line with analog pedals, medium at rate 1: mean 15.1 laps of 5 km (75.3 km), median 15.5, range 11.5-18.6. Keyboard-style driving, with every brake and throttle at 100 %, wears faster: mean 10.9.
- **Test lap (Albert Park, 5.28 km),** laps until the most worn tyre reaches 100 %, with 4 %-grip-loss laps in brackets:

| Compound | ×1 | ×2 | ×5 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Soft | 7.5 (5.6) | 3.7 | 1.5 |
| Medium | 15.0 (11.3) | 7.5 | 3.0 |
| Hard | 30.0 (22.5) | 15.0 | 6.0 |

- **Through the car (medium, rate 1)**, lap time against the best lap: +0.00 s up to lap 4, +0.08 at lap 8, +0.56 at lap 12, +2.2 at lap 14, then the cliff at lap 15 with a rear-left puncture.

Golden rule. I patched a copy of the v5 car in memory to apply the grip multipliers and call update() every step, then drove it next to the untouched car. On 7 tracks the two cars are bit-identical at every step while the multipliers are exactly 1. That lasts 2.3-2.9 laps from the start of timing at rate 1, and all 3 laps at Monaco and Bahrain. Lap times match to the millisecond through lap 3.

################ track.js pit lane

== summary
Every one of the 40 tracks now has a pit lane in js/track.js (track.pit + track.update). All required checks pass. One exception: at Monaco the entry line is 22 m after the start/finish line instead of before it.

How the lane is laid out: it follows the centreline as an offset curve, up to 250 m before and after the line, and gets shorter where a bend or another part of the circuit is in the way. From the track outwards: runoff, a pit wall (0.8 m thick, open at the entry and exit tapers), a 7 m lane, a strip of 16 boxes, and a 6 m garage face that is the outer wall. The tapers are C2 S-curves of 60-80 m. The side comes from the scenery-data 'pit' buildings when they exist (checked to be next to the line)

Room rule: the whole complex stays 1.5 m inside the halfway line between this straight and any other road or the inside of a bend. So it never folds, never touches another part of the circuit, and nearest() / locate() / inCorridor() / groundY() needed no changes.

If a layout does not fit, the code tries in this order: less runoff in front of the pit wall, then 60 km/h (8 m/s² at 80 km/h, 12 m/s² at 60), then a narrow cross-section (6 m lane), then a last resort where the entry line may come after the start line. Monaco is the only track that needs the last resort: its line sits in Casino Square between a 30 m and a 25 m radius bend.

On the racing surface nothing changed. Every sample field is identical to git HEAD except the pit-side wall distance and flag inside the lane, which only move outwards. Grid, crossings, road and paint vertices, and locate / nearest / surfaceY / groundY / inCorridor on the road all match the v5 baseline, and the racing line is identical. Build time goes up about 5-10% (+4-6 ms per track). The layout search itself costs 0.2 ms; the rest is about 4.5% more vertices through the existing buffer code.

The front wing has 3 to 5 cambered elements plus endplates (pylons on the high-nose styles). The front suspension has wishbones, a push rod and a track rod.
